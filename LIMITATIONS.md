# Scope, assumptions and limits of reproducibility

This package recreates **calculation steps possible from published summary statistics**. It does not reproduce Date Psychology's original participant-level survey nor provide novel evidence about concealed sexual histories.

## Source information and transcriptions

Each CSV documents its source and section, with URLs centralized in `source_index.csv`. Numbers are **transcribed from the specified sources**, including contradictory observations. Some percentages reflect survey weights and are rounded; no inference should be made from summing rounded percentages to 100 exactly.

Date Psychology’s article reports 628 total participants, yet counts 140 with more “had sex” partners, 57 with more oral-sex partners, and 443 with matching counts. These sum to 640. It also reports 12 women in the oral-greater group, a 12.7% male rate, and conflicting descriptions of its zero-case subgroup. We do not attempt to reconstruct an original, error-free dataset.

## Inferential reconstruction

The three gender comparisons use `scipy.stats.fisher_exact(..., alternative='two-sided')` on a 2×2 count table. The women's numerator and denominator (12 and 181) and men's denominator (447) are held fixed; the male numerator is varied among 33, 45, 57 to match different incompatible statements. None is a verified corrected input.

The correlation confidence intervals apply the approximate Fisher-*z* transformation `atanh(r) ± 1.95996/sqrt(n-3)`, then `tanh()`; they ignore uncertainty introduced by original *r* rounding and require that *n* equals the stated full overall/female sample sizes. The published article does not furnish complete variable-specific missingness counts.

The maximum SD for an interval `[1,3]` and `n=12` is an **upper bound**, using the unbiased sample denominator `n-1`; values including `1` and `3` attain the bound when evenly split. The bound is already incompatible with the reported value before imposing the rounded subgroup mean of 2.1.

The difference-of-subgroup-means calculation assumes that the report of 57 oral-greater participants includes 12 women and 45 men. It is an internally coherent scenario for those fields only; it is inconsistent with other published fields.

No power calculation, proportion test, meta-analytic model, or regression here uses participant-level data. Published group averages and meta-analytic estimates are **transcribed**, not recalculated from original records. For Figures 1, 4 and 5, the software redraws reported values; it does not refit the studies.

## Measurement and sampling caveats

- Two lists of partners can coincide in size without containing the same people.
- Oral-partner and “had sex” partner counts do not directly measure misreporting, and neither an equal count nor a differing count establishes deliberate deception.
- The denominator for the apparent absence of young women who report oral sex without “having sex” is not specified in a way that permits a faithful replication of the original 2022 comparison.
- Den Haese & King’s significant social-desirability difference is an **association**, not proof that those students knowingly lied.
- Survey-mode response ratios represent **differences in affirmative reporting**, not known errors or verified disclosure truthfulness.
- The Natsal and YouGov figures describe UK survey samples and exact question wording, not the frequency of concealed partners in the Date Psychology survey.

## Reproducibility boundaries

What is reproducible: all data transformations, sensitivity tables, sample-standard-deviation bound, approximate confidence intervals and six figures generated by the script.

What is **not** reproducible: original 2023 recruitment, exclusions, raw survey distributions, actual 2×2 gender table, nonreported eligible denominators, unreported regression adjustments, or whether individual survey answers were true. Those require original study materials not available here.

## YouGov inconsistency

The original tabulation for oral stimulation of the penis lists **45% yes**, **41% no** overall. Its accompanying news summary reverses the proportions. The CSV and Figure 6 follow the clearly labeled original table, with other response categories retained in the CSV.

## Calculation environment

Python 3.10+ with the three libraries in `requirements.txt`. For published numeric reconstruction, the script uses no random number generation or simulation; PNG rasterization may vary slightly by matplotlib and font backend versions.
